Extenders

Cards a good line goes through — the bridge to the follow-up (the material that makes the rank 6, the card that mills). Every try leans toward using them, each hand's best board is asked for again through each one, and of two equal boards the one that used more of them wins.

how extenders work A monster counts as used when the line summons it, a spell or trap when it is activated. The asked-for tries are strict: a line that reaches the board without summoning (or resolving) the extender does not count as solving them. Mark the few cards that open the follow-up, not every card in the deck — too many and every try is pulled in every direction at once.

what this curve is, and is not Each point is the pool's mean of every hand's best line SO FAR, so it can only rise or stay flat — that is what "keeps the best" means, not evidence of anything else. It rises when the search finds a board it had not found before. When it flattens, the remaining hands are at the best this solver finds at this budget, which is not proof they cannot do better. The solver itself does not get smarter; the deck's known lines do.

"Total" interruptions count a card in hand only if the combo put it there: a hand trap you opened with and kept is the same card whatever you play, so it is not part of a line's score (changed 2026-10-31; earlier generations' "mean interruptions" counted it).
